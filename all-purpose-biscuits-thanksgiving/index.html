All-Purpose Biscuits (Thanksgiving, Makes 12)

A plain, tall, flaky biscuit, doubled from our test batch so there are about 2 each for 5 adults. Made ahead and frozen, then baked straight from frozen. Adapted from All-Purpose Biscuits by Sam Sifton, NYT Cooking.

12 biscuitsAbout 4 to 6 g per biscuit protein per servingPrep 45 minCook 20 minVegetarianGinger-freeMake-aheadHoliday

Ingredients

  • 510 g all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • 4 tbsp baking powder, about 48 g
  • 2 scant tbsp sugar, about 22 g
  • 2 tsp fine salt
  • 140 g cold unsalted butter, European style
  • 480 ml cold whole milk

Steps

  1. PrepCut the butter into thin pats and keep them in the fridge until needed. Line a sheet pan that fits in your freezer with parchment and flour your work surface well.
  2. Mix the dry ingredientsSift the flour, baking powder, sugar and salt together, then tip them into a food processor.
  3. Cut in the butterAdd the butter and pulse 5 or 6 times, just until the mix looks like coarse crumbs with pea-sized bits of butter still showing. If your processor is smaller than 11 cups, do this in two batches.
  4. Add the milkPut the mix in a large bowl, pour in the milk and stir with a fork only until it comes together in a shaggy ball.
  5. FoldTurn the dough onto the floured surface and pat it into a rectangle about 2.5 cm thick. Fold it in half and pat it flat again. Do this 3 times in all.
  6. RestCover loosely with a kitchen towel and let the dough rest for 30 minutes.
  7. CutPat the dough out to about 30 by 25 cm. Cut straight down with a floured biscuit cutter and lift it out without twisting, since twisting seals the edges and stops the rise.
  8. FreezeSet the biscuits on the lined sheet pan and freeze for about 1 hour, until firm. Then move them to a gallon freezer bag.
  9. Bake (Thursday)Heat the oven to 425°F. Bake straight from frozen on a parchment-lined sheet pan for 13 to 20 minutes, until golden brown. Serve warm.

Notes

Make ahead: steps 1 to 8 on Wednesday, about 1¼ hours including the rest and the freeze. Step 9 on Thursday, no thawing.

Swaps (only if needed; don't buy both): a floured drinking glass instead of a biscuit cutter (the edge is duller, so they rise a little less). A pastry cutter or two forks instead of the food processor.

Unbaked extras keep in the freezer for a month.

Nutrition: white flour and butter make these a holiday-only bake.

Source: adapted from All-Purpose Biscuits by Sam Sifton, NYT Cooking.
