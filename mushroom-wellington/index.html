Miso-Chestnut Mushroom Wellington

Adapted from Vegetarian Mushroom Wellington (NYT Cooking). Our changes: miso replaces the soy sauce, toasted chestnuts replace the walnuts, dry sherry replaces the port, and a miso gravy replaces the port reduction. The gravy has its own page (Miso Gravy, triple batch), so this page covers the Wellington only.

8 servingsAbout 9 g protein per servingPrep 30 minCook 150 minVegetarianMake-aheadHoliday

Ingredients

  • 4 large portobello mushrooms, about 250 g (portobellos)
  • 75 ml extra virgin olive oil (portobellos)
  • Kosher salt (portobellos)
  • Black pepper (portobellos)
  • 900 g mixed mushrooms (filling)
  • 125 ml extra virgin olive oil, divided (filling)
  • 140 g steamed peeled chestnuts (filling)
  • 4 shallots, about 150 g (filling)
  • 6 garlic cloves (filling)
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary (filling)
  • 80 ml dry sherry (filling)
  • 35 g white miso (filling)
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (filling)
  • Ice, for cooling (filling)
  • 45 g unsalted butter (caramelized onions)
  • 2 small yellow onions (caramelized onions)
  • ¾ tsp brown sugar (caramelized onions)
  • 1¼ tsp kosher salt (caramelized onions)
  • ¾ tsp black pepper (caramelized onions)
  • 240 ml apple cider (caramelized onions)
  • Optional: 15 ml aged balsamic vinegar (caramelized onions)
  • 400 g all-butter puff pastry, thawed (pastry)
  • Flour, for dusting (pastry)
  • 1 large egg (pastry)

Steps

  1. PrepWipe the portobellos with a damp paper towel. Pull off the stems, then slice off the rim that curls over the gills so the caps sit flat. Keep the stems and trimmings. Trim the mixed mushrooms. Finely chop the 4 shallots, 6 garlic cloves and rosemary. Slice the onions into 6 mm rounds. Chop the chestnuts to chickpea size. Whisk the miso into the sherry. Beat the egg.
  2. Sear the portobellosBrush the caps on both sides with 45 ml oil and season well. Heat 30 ml oil in a large nonstick skillet over medium-high. Sear gill side down for 5 minutes, flip and sear 5 minutes more. Cool gill side down on a wire rack so the juices drain.
  3. Chop the fillingRoughly chop two-thirds of the mixed mushrooms, then pulse them in batches in a food processor to 6 to 12 mm pieces. Chop the rest by hand, along with the portobello stems and trimmings, to about 6 mm. Combine in a large bowl.
  4. Toast the chestnutsWipe the skillet. Toast the chestnuts in 5 ml of the filling oil over medium-high for 4 to 5 minutes, until browned in spots. Set aside.
  5. Cook the fillingHeat 60 ml oil over medium-high. Add half the mushrooms, shallots, garlic and rosemary, season, and cook for about 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until browned and tender. Move to a bowl. Repeat with the remaining 60 ml oil and ingredients.
  6. Deglaze and coolReturn the first batch to the skillet. Add the sherry-miso mix and thyme and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, until the liquid is gone. Tip into the bowl and stir in the chestnuts. Set the bowl over an ice bath and stir now and then for at least 20 minutes, until cool.
  7. Caramelize the onionsWipe the skillet and melt the butter over medium. Add the onions, sugar, salt and pepper and cook for 5 minutes. Add the cider and cook for about 15 minutes, stirring now and then, until the liquid is gone and the onions are caramelized. Stir in the balsamic if using. Cool.
  8. Roll the pastryHeat the oven to 400°F. Roll the pastry on lightly floured parchment to 33 by 40 cm. Slide the parchment onto a sheet pan with a long side facing you.
  9. LayerSpread half the filling in a 10 by 25 cm strip down the center, leaving 4 cm clear at each end. Lay the onions on top in a 7.5 cm strip. Line up the portobellos on the onions, stem side down, trimming any that are too big. Cover with the rest of the filling and pack it into a loaf shape.
  10. WrapFold one long side of pastry over the filling to almost cover it. Brush that pastry with egg, then fold the other side over it to make a double layer and press to seal. Brush the inside of the short ends with egg, press and tuck them under. Trim any overhanging parchment. It can be refrigerated for up to 1 day at this point.
  11. BakeBrush the top with egg. Score a light crosshatch through the top layer only. Bake for 45 to 50 minutes, until deep golden and flaky. Rest on the pan for 10 minutes.
  12. ServeCut crosswise into 8 slabs so each shows the portobello in cross-section. Serve cut side up with the miso gravy (see the Miso Gravy page).

Notes

Swaps (only if needed; don't buy both): port instead of dry sherry, same amount, or 15 to 30 ml aged balsamic. Roasted chestnuts instead of steamed; skip the toasting step. Apple juice instead of apple cider, same amount.

Make ahead: the filling and onions keep 2 days in the fridge; skip the ice bath and just chill them. The assembled, unbaked Wellington keeps 1 day. Baked straight from the fridge, it needs about 5 extra minutes.

Gravy: made separately from the Miso Gravy page (triple batch, about 1.1 liters). For a single batch, about 360 ml, use a third of every amount.

Ginger check: read the label on the miso.
