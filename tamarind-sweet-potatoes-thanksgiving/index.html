Fiery Sweet Potatoes with Tamarind and Lime (Thanksgiving for 5)

The tested recipe scaled up by half for Thanksgiving, nothing else changed. A coconut and chile sweet potato mash under a sour tamarind glaze, finished with chile-toasted pepitas. The homemade chile paste keeps it ginger-free.

6 servingsAbout 7 g protein per servingPrep 20 minCook 75 minVegetarianGinger-freeMake-aheadHoliday

Ingredients

  • 3 Fresno chiles (chile paste)
  • 2 small garlic cloves (chile paste)
  • 1 shallot, about 45 g (chile paste)
  • 1½ lemongrass stalks (chile paste)
  • ¾ tsp ground coriander (chile paste)
  • ¼ tsp plus a pinch ground cumin (chile paste)
  • Zest of 1½ limes (chile paste)
  • 7 ml extra virgin olive oil, 1½ tsp (chile paste)
  • Pinch of kosher salt (chile paste)
  • 1.35 kg sweet potatoes, 4 to 5 medium (mash)
  • 180 ml full-fat coconut milk (mash)
  • 45 g unsalted butter (mash)
  • 1 tsp plus a pinch kosher salt (mash)
  • 90 g Goya frozen tamarind pulp, thawed (glaze)
  • 45 ml pure maple syrup (glaze)
  • 22 g unsalted butter (glaze)
  • Juice of 1½ limes (glaze)
  • Pinch of kosher salt (glaze)
  • 70 g raw pepitas (topping)
  • 7 ml extra virgin olive oil, 1½ tsp (topping)
  • ¼ tsp plus a pinch red pepper flakes (topping)
  • Optional: extra lime zest (topping)
  • Optional: cilantro leaves (topping)

Steps

  1. PrepScrub and prick the sweet potatoes. Stem the chiles (seed one for milder heat). Peel the garlic and shallot. Use only the tender inner part of the lemongrass, thinly sliced. Zest the limes, then juice them. Pick cilantro leaves if using.
  2. Bake the potatoesHeat the oven to 400°F. Bake the sweet potatoes on a foil-lined sheet pan for 60 to 75 minutes, until completely soft. Cool slightly and peel.
  3. Chile pasteBlend or pound the chiles, garlic, shallot, lemongrass, coriander, cumin, lime zest, oil and salt until mostly smooth. Warm the paste in a small saucepan for 1 minute, add the coconut milk and simmer for 3 minutes.
  4. MashMash the potatoes with the coconut mixture, butter and salt. Taste for heat and salt. Spread in a 28 x 18 cm (11 x 7 in) baking dish. Cool, cover and refrigerate.
  5. PepitasToast the pepitas in the oil over medium heat for 3 to 4 minutes, until they pop and brown. Toss with the pepper flakes and a pinch of salt. Cool and keep airtight at room temperature.
  6. GlazeSimmer the tamarind, maple syrup, butter, lime juice and salt in a small pan for 3 to 4 minutes, stirring, until thick enough to coat a spoon. Cool and refrigerate in a jar.
  7. Reheat (Thursday)Take the dish out of the fridge about an hour ahead. Cover with foil and bake at 400°F for about 35 minutes (40 if straight from the fridge), until hot through.
  8. Glaze and broilRewarm the glaze over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until loose. Uncover the dish, brush with the glaze and broil for 2 to 4 minutes, watching closely, until it bubbles and darkens at the edges.
  9. FinishScatter the pepitas, a little extra lime zest and the cilantro over the top just before serving.

Notes

Make ahead: steps 1 to 6 on Wednesday. The mash keeps a day in the fridge, the glaze longer. Steps 7 to 9 on Thursday.

Swaps (only if needed; don't buy both): 45 g seedless tamarind block pulp instead of the Goya frozen pulp; warm the glaze only about 2 minutes, then press it through a strainer. 2 Fresno chiles instead of 3 for a milder dish.

Dish: a 23 cm (9 in) square dish also works. A 23 x 33 cm (9 x 13 in) dish spreads the mash thin.

Coconut milk: one 400 ml can covers this with about 220 ml left over. Freeze the rest.

Nutrition: about 7 ml maple syrup per serving, the same as the test. Standouts: vitamin A, fiber, and magnesium and zinc from the pepitas.
